# References and Cybersecurity Guidance

## Overview

This project uses authoritative cybersecurity guidance from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).

These sources informed the assessment of password security, multifactor authentication, phishing resistance, password managers, and stronger authentication approaches.

---

## Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)

### Require Multifactor Authentication

**Organization:** Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, U.S. Department of Homeland Security

CISA recommends multifactor authentication as an important account-security control because an attacker who obtains a password must still satisfy an additional authentication requirement.

The guidance also emphasizes stronger and phishing-resistant forms of MFA.

**Source:**  
https://www.cisa.gov/audiences/small-and-medium-businesses/secure-your-business/require-multifactor-authentication

---

## National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

### NIST SP 800-63B-4 — Authentication and Authenticator Management

**Organization:** National Institute of Standards and Technology, U.S. Department of Commerce

NIST SP 800-63B provides technical guidance for authentication and authenticator management.

Relevant concepts considered in this project include:

- Password requirements
- Authentication assurance
- Authenticator management
- Phishing resistance
- Password managers
- Authentication security

**Source:**  
https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html

---

### How Do I Create a Good Password?

**Organization:** National Institute of Standards and Technology, U.S. Department of Commerce

This NIST guidance provides practical recommendations for password creation and management, including the importance of longer passwords and the use of password managers.

**Source:**  
https://www.nist.gov/cybersecurity-and-privacy/how-do-i-create-good-password

---

## Application to This Project

These resources were used to evaluate the security and usability of different authentication approaches and develop recommendations involving:

- Long, unique passwords
- Password managers
- Multifactor authentication
- Stronger MFA methods
- Phishing-resistant authentication
- Authentication usability
- Account recovery
- User security guidance

The recommendations presented in this portfolio project are an academic analysis based on these cybersecurity resources and do not represent official guidance issued by CISA, NIST, or UMGC.
